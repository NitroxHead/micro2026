Screening commercial smoked seafood for microplastics: a multi-taxa market survey

In short

Forty-five packs of smoked and other ready-to-eat seafood (15 product categories, three packs each; salmon from five anonymised suppliers plus one ASC-certified line) were bought on the Istanbul retail market, digested in 10 % KOH with 2 % Tween-20, filtered onto 0.2 µm alumina membranes and screened under a stereo microscope. Every pack carried suspected particles; fibres dominated and most particles were 1–5 mm long. The counts are first-pass, visual and not yet blank-corrected, so they are an upper bound. Polymer identification by µ-FTIR comes next, and only confirmed particles will enter the per-portion exposure estimate in the thesis.

Category means only are published here; individual pack readings stay in the thesis until it is examined.
